# AI/LLM Coding Policy - Briefing

DSA National Tech Committee

## Summary
This policy was written by DSA National Tech Committee Steering with input from dozens of our members over the summer of 2026 in an open request for comment thread on the DSA Discussion Forum with reportbacks from how they use AI coding tools in their day jobs, their DSA roles as rank and file members or local leaders, or today on the National Tech Committee.

This document is in two parts: the first: a summary of the process, our findings, and some recommended readings around AI; the second: our AI/LLM Coding Policy which will be debated at the October 4th NTC General meeting and may be amended by NTC members in the week leading up to the meeting at our amendment form here.

Our policy aims to find synthesis on approach to this class of tools and where it exists in the broader scope of socialist technology work along with what sensible safeguards for adopting its use with technology work across DSA from national committees such as ours along with this being an easy to adopt template for NTC members to bring back to their chapters to align with the NTC on AI use.

## Writing Methodology

First pass of this document and the bulk of the policy suggestions was written with input from NTC members and leaders in our request for comment thread to address a median determined position of these comments as feedback ranged from outright bans to encouragement of use with no restrictions. Members lead this organization and we wanted this policy, along with any restrictions of use, to be set democratically. Many of the comments and discussions around this policy have come up in the last several months around increasing AI usage both inside of DSA for technology work as well as in our day jobs and the surrounding world. In other words, it’s a spectre haunting much of our daily life.

For cases where conflicting feedback was gathered (e.g. Only use AI on certain projects vs Use AI everywhere), we selected the more popular of the two items from the feedback thread. In cases where there were unique positions, we included them only if they did not otherwise conflict with the majority sentiment.

A second pass of this document was done incorporating recent policies from similar community run open source projects who are also dealing with the question of how to incorporate AI tooling into their contributors. Like DSA many of these projects are democratically run or rely on heavy community and contributor feedback to set their project’s governance and are also dealing with a similar influx of AI based contributions. Projects like Godot, Rust, GNU GCC, Codeberg and Debian, among others, have adopted policies prior to or in parallel of the NTC’s assessment that we also need to draft one for our members (Debian’s in particular, came from a several week open discussion and subsequent two week voting period). Much of this has come from the urgency of a number of new LLM based commits or an overwhelming number of PRs leading to maintainer frustration and burnout – in one such recent case, the curl project recently went on a month long vacation of zero development or merging PR requests (including security fixes) due to the overwhelming nature of these requests. These external organizations have not set policy for the NTC but helped shape the suggestions being put forward.

Our third and final pass writing this document was the NTC Steering Committee drafting amendments between its 2026-09-04 and 2026-09-24 Steering meetings. A summary of amendments put forward by NTC SC members may be found here.

As much of the feedback and research is developed from individual or externally determined opinions, we’ve done our best to distill them into unopinionated positions where possible.

## Findings
A majority of positions expressed finding some value in use of AI for coding, with clear requests for guidelines for how to use AI tools, how to implement them for DSA NTC or Chapter projects, which tools are preferred (which providers, local or cloud based) and requests by project leaders on good governance policies around how to enforce best practices when AI generated code is submitted from members and on what DSA data/code bases are permitted for training or running AI tools against. Many members also expressed hesitation on wanting to get involved in coding projects or even one off tasks on the NTC if AI tools were unavailable, citing a lack of time or capacity to replicate work from their day jobs and in between other responsibilities they have with families, friends, and as socialist organizers in their local chapters.

Project leaders also expressed the drain of majority AI contributions on their motivation to contribute to NTC work and want to find the balance between its use and overuse. AI hallucinations, broken code, pull requests in the thousands of lines, or even a lack of understanding on what’s been deployed have all been negatives in DSA and non-DSA spaces our members do technology work in.

Some discussions also occurred around if it’s better to use commercial AI tools like Claude or ChatGPT for DSA use or if we should begin exploring and hosting our own AI infrastructure with local models, but no definitive conclusion was reached.

Minority positions included full bans on AI/LLM use for coding applications, citing energy usage, theft of intellectual property or without attribution by training data, right wing positions & collaboration with the Trump regime, and whether these tools are hype from the capitalist class to punish labor. An opposing minority position also included full unrestricted use of AI tools on the NTC citing demands from local chapters to develop and deploy tools to compete at the scale that our class enemies are competing at with the few developers and development hours we have.

Other findings were on the more philosophical side of AI use: what does it mean to be a technologist, let alone socialist technologists that are fighting to own the means of production, if comrades are outsourcing our thinking to algorithms? Questions were also raised about how this policy may align with or contradict policies adopted in chapters like Chicago DSA or DSA National’s No AI Images resolution.

## Closing Thoughts & Suggested Reading List
As we approach the October 4th 2026 General Meeting where our members will discuss and debate this policy, we’d like to ground this discussion around the political understanding of AI and its use with more general questions of the state of computing in relation to the political economy. To start to tackle thinking about this issue a bit beyond the technical aspects and more into the social aspects: is AI use truly freeing up our time as technologists to focus on improving our movement or our craft? Or is this a way to accelerate the division of labor at record scale to further alienate us from our skills?

A number of NTC members have expressed over the years privately and most recently in our AI request for comment thread that they’d rather not think about or spend time on technology inside the context of DSA, or even hold skepticism over whether this area of work has a place in the movement. There’s a number of reasons why some hold this and not any single one can cleanly map to individuals that’ve expressed this feeling: burnout at our day jobs and the alienation we feel from our labor; to the increasing rise of responsibilities from us as we move through phases in life: we graduate, fall in love, maybe start a family, find hobbies; seeing the curiosity in computing disappear with less incentive to understand how something works, just that it does on tighter and tighter deadlines. All while we feel the crunch while trying to change our material conditions by organizing in our communities and our workplaces in worse conditions.

# NTC AI/LLM Usage Policy
We write this policy in the context of AI upending both the tech industry and technology practitioners in the last several years. The capitalist class uses the promise of AI’s magical technology to punish workers with the looming threat of layoffs, increase in work, and mandating AI be shoehorned into every aspect of our work lives. In spite of this, NTC members have also found good use and applications for this technology in organizing contexts or in DSA to help accelerate software work.

The NTC neither endorses nor prohibits the use of generative AI tools in the development, or maintenance of software written for DSA. We recognize that such tools can substantially improve the productivity of contributors when used responsibly, allowing volunteers to spend more of their limited time on work that requires technical expertise, judgment, review, and collaboration. We provide these following points of guidance around the use of AI tools to help enable our members to do good work for our committee and our organization.

## Acceptable Use & Member Expectations

- Members are expected to disclose when they use AI and for what it was used for. Members may be requested to explain in detail what code they’ve submitted does either in text or in meetings.
- Good faith is to be assumed for members using AI as well as for members not using AI, what matters is the quality of the contribution. Bad faith accusations of AI use or attempts to smear other members over AI use (or lack thereof) will be seen as a code of conduct violation. 

## Project Maintainer Expectations
- Contribution triaging responsibilities have not changed – regardless of how it was written: code, configs, or other AI generated output are expected to be assessed equally; namely passing tests & security reviews.
- Project maintainers are expected to make upfront disclosure of AI use part of their projects, either via pull request templates or some other way of encouraging members to openly state when they use it.
- Project maintainers will be responsible for enforcement of good faith expectations around AI use and create a welcoming culture for new contributors.
- No official NTC work shall require the use of machine learning models or license to machine learning models that are proprietary or have been trained on proprietary data in order to contribute to that project.

## Unacceptable Use
- Code documentation (excluding: in-code comments and commit messages), correspondence (including but not limited to: tickets, pull requests, forum, email, or chats), or other governance documents must be written by members.
- Using DSA data for training any Generative AI models unless otherwise approved by NTC Steering is prohibited.
- Following R02: No AI Images!, any artwork, graphics, or images used in NTC developed software must not be AI generated.

## Provider Selection
- Following the spirit of R49: Resolution on the Use of X.com - AI algorithms and tools provided by Twitter/X.com/xAI are prohibited and contributions from these tools must be rejected.
- We encourage members to look to R09: For Political and Technological Independence: A Path to Exit from Capitalist and Democratic Party Tech for inspiration on which providers to use:
  - Prohibition on any AI providers if they are BDS targets
  - Investigate feasibility of “better” providers (if any exist) per our software rubric, namely: 
    - Unionized staff or run by a worker cooperative
    - Open Source/Open Weight (if possible)
    - Self-Hosted (where feasible)
    - Provider has zero data retention (ZDR) for any models that will be used - no prompts, inputs, or any other data should be retained by the provider unless explicitly provided by the user
  - NTC Steering will review any request for licenses of AI tools using the same procurement procedure as any other technology request for approval, or recommend alternatives if rejected.

## Policy Updates
- This policy may be amended by members at our quarterly general meetings.
- If DSA Convention or NPC passes any restrictions around AI tools that impact this policy, NTC Steering will be responsible for rectifying conflicting policy items and announce to NTC membership.
