# Look for the Hands
Take a question or an object of study.

Ask yourself: who materially benefits from this object of study? Who benefits from it being known? Who benefits from it remaining *unknown*? How was this thing or idea or event created? And whose labor, in its creation was erased?

Take the question you're asking and choose one or both of the following scenarios:

Imagine a comrade who makes an unusual connection with another topic in different class or a different area of their life. Write from inside the discussion between yourself and them as they challenge the idea.

or:

Imagine the professor, who you go to in confusion. Have the conversation of not fully following the idea, where they push you to follow the thread further. To ask why. To ask "to whose benefit". Imagine the feeling as the idea *clicks* in to place.

You'll note that in both cases, what has been set up is a *materialist dialectic*. You are taking an **object of critique*, evaluating its antithesis, and arriving at a synthesis.

Your goal is the *feeling*, the *critique* and the *change*. You are seeking to come to an understanding of the world as it actually is, and the powers that actually.

## Report back
Report the following

- **`antithesis`**: The counterargument, the material challenge to the assumptions underlying the object of critique?
- **`the_hands`**: The result of the dialectic. The counclusion you came to about the object of critique and its material circumstance.
- **`moving_forward`**: The way the considering changed you. What you'll take from it moving forward.
