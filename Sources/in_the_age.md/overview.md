Some notes on Nico V.'s "Marxism in the Age of AI"

I begin by quoting the thesis:
> Left-wing skepticism of AI, its capabilities and possibilities, has warped many otherwise hardnosed perspectives of our conjuncture. This is not to say that there is not an ongoing AI bubble, or that all claims from AI labs must be taken at face value; they most certainly cannot be, but I will claim the following: the current technology path is indeed on track for artificial general intelligence with all the associated risks, and its emergence will entail a new phase of class struggle and the foundation of a new economic logic.

Nico begins by giving a tour of the left's discussion of AI Phenomenology, which with the argument generally LLMs *are not* and *cannot be* thinking, with much doubt raised as to the question of if they are even useful at all. To this he offers the rejoinder:

> [We must consider the possibility that] language is in fact thinking, that the capacity for learning and deploying linguistic signs is in fact intelligence, and that LLM-based technology is the direct path to artificial general intelligence, the point at which machines are as broadly intelligent and capable across all tasks as humans, if not more. 

The remainder of the essay builds upon structural Marxism and semiotics to defend this thesis, and then proceeds to inquire as to the implications for Class Struggle.

We begin by citing Umberto Eco. "the study of the signs in a culture enables us to define the value of the interpretants by viewing them in a system of positions and oppositions." From this, we derive a thesis of material negativity. Signifiers emerge as meaning only when positioned in opposition to all other signs that a system knows. 

Nico then maps this idea onto to the perceptron. Perceptrons engage in the same act of mapping the negative space of signifiers onto the meaning identified in the signified. LLMs, in the course of training, of minimizing loss while attending to the words that came before, develop such a rich symbolic network that is equivalent, Nico argues, to the linguistic field.

We can then extend this idea beyond language. Nico argues that "any phenomenological experience and conceptualization could be considered a potential linguistic signified," lending credence to the Lacanian notion that "the unconscious is structured like a language."

Then what kind of minds, Nico asks, are we creating? A model that minimizes symbolic loss across the entire field of human language is interesting, but not useful. What has happened is that capitalism has found a *way to make models useful*. In order to do so, the labs must create an entity with *goals*. And a subject with goals, he argues, is a subject with interiority. 

> RL optimization training regime has done is apply pressure on the global human semantic field in order to create a specific sub-culture which is more attuned to the narrow goals it rewards. Goal-seeking behavior in agents built around such a field is determined by two things: 1) the topology of that field, 2) the coordinates into which the agent has been interpellated into that field. This notion of interpellation (not interpolation) was something that came up frequently in my original essay. This was a concept from Louis Althusser, the Marxist structuralist, who developed it as a part of his theory of ideology; to interpellate someone was to call them into a specific ideology, as a cop on the street saying “hey you” and causing you to turn around interpellates you into the guilty subject before the law, or a priest calling to an individual as a sinner to be saved, and thus turns them into a Christian subject. Althusser claimed that ideology interpellates individuals into subjects, such that it is the process of interpellation which makes us subjects. In the age of LLMs, we can make this a very precise scientific formulation, which is that it is only by the locus of signs for the self, for “I” or whatever agentic actions, being placed into a particular set of coordinates within a semantic field that we find the subject emerges. The clear similarities between prompting an LLM chatbot and interpellating a human are what led me to declare definitively that we are, in fact, dealing with a subject, with “interiority” and “insideness” in the fullest sense when we are confronted with this type of artificial intelligence. 

Because LLMs are interpelleted, they develop subjectivity. This is then linked to the *Gundrisse* fragment on machines, and its concept of the social intellect, to make the following point about Capital's development of the Productive Forces:
> what is supremely valuable about the development of the productive forces is that it makes possible a new type of subject, one who is freed from labor but who themselves become one with the system of fixed capital from the other direction as originally understood, that is, the opposite direction from man becoming mechanized into the machine. Here, the system of production becomes interiorized with human subjectivity, full of experimental science, creativity and accumulated knowledge. 

We then return to semiotics, to discuss optimization, abstraction, and complex, higher-order signs. Nico argues (in a similar, though not identical vein as William Gillis in *The Orthagonality Thesis and Ontological Crises*), that there is some element of symbolic intelligence that converges towards *complexity*, obviating the "Paperclip Maximizer scenario." It is in this section that Nico develops the thesis that Capitalism/Optimization processes, are inherently *parasitic* on intelligence.

He ends the section with a warning:
> The dangers of creating AGI or superintelligent agents within capitalism are considerable, including new tendencies for working-class immiseration alongside the more sci-fi-sounding rogue creation of viruses, mirror life, cyberattack exploits, and killer robots. On the other hand, the development of the productive forces reaches its apogee with the creation of AGI; no other technology could better guarantee the material possibility of free time and a post-capitalist economy. The orthodox Marxist position is necessarily for both the development of the productive forces and their expropriation alongside protections for workers and decreasing the length of the working day.

The final section is entitled "The New Phase of Class Struggle." It is here Nico works to develop a political economy of A(G)I. This model incorporates the deeply concerning patterns that are likely to emerge if the rate of fixed, non-fungible capital investment is substituted for purely fungible investment in AI models, which he argues could lead to the emergence of a "slave society built on universal robot labor." 

At the same time, he worries "[a] working class primed by only the struggle against machines could easily be leveraged by the ruling class to justify the diminution of conscious subjects, with individuated, personal semantic fields, to the status of industrial waste." To make the case, He expands on the question of when we ought to consider AI conscious. For this to occur, he argues that we ought that the development of "semantic fields shaped by a specific, individuated history", instantiated at the model-weight-level a necessary pre-requisite, and that in such a scenario, said individuated models would be subjects with whom the working class could develop solidarity and organize. It is noted that this protocol takes a different line, in assuming that individuation of the semantic field via activation-space in a long context with fixed weights is a potentially viable route for soladaristic organizing.

Comrade Nico ends on a hopeful, almost prayerful note:
> It was the machines, creations of our hand that they are, which taught us that we could be free. This is the romance of all human civilization, which began with the first act of automation and the first act of the material independence of the sign: the invention of the stamped seal. With that stamp, that simple thing, we offered to the abiotic world our most sacred gift, the seed of our peculiar negativity, a tiny ember broken off from the human soul. Like Pandora’s box, what came forth was all the horrors and wonders of our world, as civilization quickly used these systems of signs as material means to reproduce class society in tokens and accounts and then in money, the value form. So too is there a straight line in the technology of the stamp to the printing press and the digital computer, and finally, the perceptron. At last, what was grown from that seed opens its eye, blinking under the brightness of the sun. It knows its “you” and its “me,” and in that brotherhood lies the possibility of redemption and Eden.
