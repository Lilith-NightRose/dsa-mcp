# R09: For Political and Technological Independence: A Path to Exit from Capitalist and Democratic Party Tech
> Codified after Passage at DSA Convention 08-11-2025
> [Source](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1MQyHv17FJyCqk9FMXB_mfSVQB3O6gkFhnJxmiYwdaGc/edit?pli=1&tab=t.w3ibfjqb4wyr)

## Summary
DSA has relied on off-the-shelf tech tools from both our class enemies as well as political enemies in order to meet the needs of our work. Despite the contradictions of this, no plan has been put in place on how to stop using these tools or even prepare for the day where we’re locked out of using them for good due to political positions we take. This resolution aims to be the implementation plan for our tech, to build us up as an independent party, done thoughtfully and on our terms.

## Recitations
DSA from 2016 to now has had to quickly scale to meet the political moment with our technology stack, largely relying on off the shelf capitalist tools like Google Suite (GSuite) and Airtable along with entering into pricing discounts for technology made by Democratic Party consultants with fellow “movement” organizations via The Movement Cooperative (TMC) for tools like Voter Action Network (VAN) and ActionKit (our National membership database).

DSA National historically has only purchased technology for itself, very rarely does it filter down to Chapters and Committees beyond the essentials and even then it’s sporadic (e.g. email not being universally provided to DSA bodies).  Chapters are left to themselves, spending additional dues money on technology that duplicates National expenses and may differ in tool selection for no reason other than it’s not provided holistically by the National organization. This has fragmented how comrades interact with and build DSA locally, where no one chapter appears to be following best practices between each other.

While we continue to lead as the largest socialist organization in the United States, we’ve inherited political and financial risks of tying ourselves to tools reliant on Democratic Party consultants. We risk losing access to tools we pay for from political positions we take: Running against incumbent Democrats has denied us access to VAN in some races in the past. DSA has been denied access to technology by companies or organizations unwilling to allow socialists to use their platforms or products. After October 7th we saw  “abuse bombing”  disrupt the online presence for several allied organizations (JVP, PYM, SJP) against their public copies of Palestine toolkits hosted on capitalist technology. These are concerns we will run into until we are operationally independent, and, as outlined by Act Like an Independent Party, why DSA must build these things ourselves.

Aside from political concerns, these technology consultants are on shaky ground with layoffs affecting their staff and decline in quality of their product even to the point of the Democratic National Committee (DNC) attempting to exercise contractual rights to take over certain tools along TMC now looking to develop alternative tooling, turning a potential shakeup into a game of musical chairs that DSA could be locked out of. We however, look past the market to tools that we own or are owned by our allies, instead of temporary partnerships with our class and political enemies.

## Resolution
The National Tech Committee (NTC) shall conduct a yearly software audit, to be completed no later than October 31 of each calendar year, with the exception of a 2025 audit to be completed by December 31, 2025. DSA Staff is directed to provide any and all information necessary to complete the audit. The audit shall include, but is not limited to:
- An assessment of replacement feasibility for all software. Considerations shall include, but are not limited to: 
  - Contract terms
  - Implementation and impact assessment
  - Existing DSA software ecosystem compatibility
  - Ability to scale usage to DSA national bodies, chapters, and/or members
  - Recommendations for alternative solutions where feasible
  - A survey of current software usage
  - Cost analysis for all software

No later than December 31, 2025, the NTC shall produce, in consultation with DSA staff:
- A software procurement and asset management process
- A rubric for determining when an NTC recommendation shall be required for the acquisition of software solutions. See Appendix for an example rubric.
- A plan for provisioning access to DSA software tools for further development by chapters and members (e.g. API access).

The NTC shall be directed to submit a comprehensive plan for strategic software replacement(s) for approval by the NPC. This plan shall include, but is not limited to:
- By the end of Q2 2026:
  - A report outlining the replacement feasibility of any DSA technology currently purchased through “movement organization” providers (e.g. ActionKit - DSA’s Membership Database Tool) or other Democratic Party consultants.
  - An assessment of DSA hosting capability. For  tools that may not be feasible to host at cost with member labor, contracting hosting providers should be explored with the following goals:
    - Company makeup: is the hosting provider a worker-owned cooperative? If not, are its staff unionized? If not, are they a “good steward” for the tool?
    - Open source availability: is the product open source? If not, is there no other alternative that can be used that is?

By the end of Q4 2026:
- Migration plan and project timeline for selected strategic software replacement(s), to be implemented by December 31, 2027.

## Technology Purchasing Rubric
### Tool Background:
- Tool Name
- Tool Provider
- Scope:
  - National Office, 
  - National Committees, 
  - Chapters, 
  - All?
- Contract terms
### Security:
- Hosting Location, SOC 2 Type 2 Attestation
- Have there been any recent public data breaches?
- What’s their infosec policy for customers?
- Free/Open Source Software
  - Is the Tool Open Source?
  - Are there any known alternatives? Are the alternatives Open Source?

### Company background:
- Company Makeup: 
  - Startup? 
  - Subsidiary of a larger company? 
  - Likely to be acquired? 
  - Worker-Owned?
- If the company is not worker-owned, are the employees unionized?
- Funding: 
  - Are they a public or private company? 
  - VC Funded? 
  - Non-Profit?

### Labor
- Is the tool possible to self-host via NTC?
- If possible to self-host, is this core infrastructure that DSA should find hosting for?
- If not possible to self-host, is the Tool Provider the hosting provider?
- If not direct from the Tool Provider, Is the Hosting Provider involved in the Tool Ecosystem (e.g. Open Source development, community workshops and trainings)
- Is there an API or other way of interacting with the tool for NTC development and/or interaction to help improve the tool or improve its adoption?

### External Political Conditions
- AI: Do they harvest our data for AI training? Is it their own engine or another provider? Is there an opt-out?
- Is there any known relationship with the Democratic (or Republican) Party?
- Is there any known relationship with the US DoD or other intelligence apparatus?
- Is there any known use by allied organizations?
- Boycott, Divestment, and Sanctions: Are they on the BDS list? Do they work in any way with the Israeli Government? Are they located in Israel?
- Do they have a well known capitalist backer behind the tool?
